It is Monday morning. You need to know what moved, what is forecasted, which deals are stale, and whether the number in front of you can be trusted. This track teaches you to answer all four in minutes — and, more importantly, to know when a number is solid enough to say out loud in front of a board.

The rest of the company uses the CRM to *run* deals. Leadership uses it to *read* them — across all three verticals at once, without asking anyone to build a report. That is a different skill, and it has one governing rule: **a dashboard total is a claim, not a fact, until you have seen the records behind it.**

## What You Are Trying To Do

This track is for reading the business, not entering every record.

You are learning how to:

1. **Read the rollup** - pipeline, won, forecast, and vertical-level dashboards.
2. **Check forecast quality** - stages, dates, values, stale deals, and missing next steps.
3. **Understand win/loss** - RFP outcomes by league, time period, and business area.
4. **Ask for a number and verify it** - use the assistant, then inspect the records behind the answer.

Each of those maps to one lesson. None of them require you to create or edit records — the verticals own their data. Your job is to read it, question it, and send it back for repair when it is not board-grade.

## Why the read pattern matters

Every number on a dashboard is an aggregate of individual deal records maintained by the team. When those records are current — right stage, right <GlossaryTerm term="bidStatus" />, real close dates, honest values — the rollup is trustworthy. When they drift, the rollup drifts with them, silently. Nothing on a dashboard warns you that a $4M deal has had no activity in ninety days; you find that by drilling in.

That is why this track drills one habit above all others: **ask fast, verify before you present.** The assistant will give you any number in one line. The dashboard will give it to you in one glance. Neither replaces opening the top five records behind a total before that total goes into a deck. Speed for exploration, records for evidence.

<Callout title="Where this lives">
Dashboards and source records live in the CRM. The assistant can summarize and calculate, but leadership numbers should be verified against the records included in the answer.
</Callout>

## Day 1 / Day 30 / Reference

- **Day 1:** read dashboard totals by vertical and open the records behind one number.
- **Day 30:** identify stale deals, missing dates, weak forecast assumptions, and win/loss patterns — and route them back to the owning team as specific asks, not general complaints.
- **Reference as needed:** exports, assistant prompts, monthly dashboard review, board-level summaries.

## Quick Glossary

- <abbr title="The higher-level lifecycle stage on a deal, such as New, Screening, Meeting, Proposal, or Customer.">Stage</abbr> - lifecycle stage on a deal.
