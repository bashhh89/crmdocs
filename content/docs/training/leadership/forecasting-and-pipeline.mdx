**After this lesson** you'll be able to read the weighted revenue forecast, explain what moves it, and identify the deals that still need Win Confidence before the number is used in a meeting.

A forecast is the most consequential number leadership touches — it drives hiring, cash planning, and what gets promised upward. It is also the easiest number to get quietly wrong, because it fails through record drift, not through math. This lesson is about knowing the difference between a forecast you can present and one you need to send back for repair.

## How the forecast works

The forecast rolls up **expected fiscal revenue across live opportunities**, weighted by each deal's **Win Confidence**. Won revenue is locked at 100%. Open revenue and margin are multiplied by the selected confidence level, so the headline moves as deals progress instead of being rebuilt by hand.

The card keeps the math visible: weighted revenue, won revenue, weighted open pipeline, weighted margin, business-unit contribution, and confidence coverage. A missing confidence is never silently guessed; it is listed as a coverage gap and contributes nothing until the record is updated.

## The click path

<WhereThisLives tool="crm" path="ANC app → Views → Revenue Forecast / Pipeline by Business Unit" />

1. Open the **2026 Won & Forecast by Business Unit** dashboard and select **2026 Forecast**.
2. Read **Weighted Revenue Forecast** first. This is won revenue at 100% plus open fiscal revenue weighted by Win Confidence.
3. Read **Won locked**, **Weighted open**, and **Weighted margin** to understand what makes up the headline.
4. Scan the business-unit bars to see where the weighted forecast is concentrated.
5. Check **Confidence coverage** and the orange notice. Open the missing deals and assign an honest Win Confidence before using the number in a meeting.
6. Use the 2027 dashboard the same way when reviewing the next fiscal year.

## Common mistakes

<OhShit
  symptom="The weighted forecast changed sharply and nobody knows why."
  fix="Ask the assistant which large deals changed status, fiscal value, or Win Confidence, then open those records. One large confidence change can explain the movement."
  prevention="Review the largest forecasted deals weekly, not at quarter-end. Swings you watch weekly are movements; swings you discover at quarter-end are surprises."
/>

Two more habits to avoid: quoting the **unweighted fiscal pipeline** as if it were the forecast, and ignoring confidence coverage. Win Confidence is still judgment encoded as a percentage — challenge it on large open deals the same way you would challenge it in conversation.

## What good looks like

- You know the current weighted forecast per business unit and roughly what drives it.
- Confidence coverage is high enough for the meeting, and you know which large deals are still missing it.
- You can explain the gap between the unweighted fiscal pipeline and the weighted forecast.
- The number you present is the number in the system — no private adjustments in a side spreadsheet.
