**After this lesson** you'll be able to see what's due this week, track a proposal through its stages, and find the estimate attached to any deal.

Pricing is where a Technology deal becomes real. Up to this point the deal is a conversation; after this point it is a number the client can sign. The estimation flow exists so that pricing work is never a private spreadsheet on someone's laptop — it lives on the deal, moves through visible stages, and every file it produces lands where the whole team can find it. This lesson covers both sides: the CRM view where the queue lives, and the Proposal Engine where the numbers get built.

## What's due this week

Your home base for proposal work is the **Estimation & Proposals** view. It shows the deals that need attention, sorted by **proposal due date**, with the columns that matter — proposal stage, priority, pricing status, who is estimating, and when the bid is due.

A deal enters this queue when it is **submitted to the estimations team** — the record carries who the assigned estimator is, when it was submitted, and even the bid due *time* when a deadline is same-day sharp ("5pm CT" matters when the upload portal closes at 5:01). If your deal needs pricing and it is not in this view, it has not actually been queued — no one is working on it, however urgent it feels.

## Proposal stages

Each deal moves through proposal stages — **RFP**, **Sales Lead**, **BAFO**, **LOI**, **Existing Client Budget** — and the view groups and triages by them. Keep the **Proposal Stage** field current so the view stays accurate and nothing slips.

- **Click it:** open the opportunity → set **Proposal Stage**. When pricing is finished, the deal shows <GlossaryTerm term="pricingComplete" /> — usually set automatically (see below).

## Where the pricing actually happens

The numbers get built in the **Proposal Engine**. The working loop looks like this:

1. Start an estimate from the deal — line items for the displays, hardware, install, spares, the lot.
2. Iterate. Every save creates a **revision**, so you can compare versions and see exactly what changed between rounds. Alternates (Alt 1 / Alt 2 / Alt 3) can be priced side by side when the client wants options.
3. Export — Excel cost sheet, one-pager, proposal PDF, or SOW.

Here is the part that keeps everything connected: every export **attaches itself to the CRM opportunity automatically** — that is <GlossaryTerm term="universalCrmPush" />. Generating the one-pager also marks the deal <GlossaryTerm term="pricingComplete" /> with the date, and sending the proposal PDF moves the bid status to Bid Submitted. When the pricing document must reproduce a source workbook exactly, line for line, that is <GlossaryTerm term="mirrorMode" /> — exact-fidelity output, no recalculated surprises.

For a fast ballpark before full pricing starts, ask Scout — the assistant has a quick-estimate skill that returns a rough figure in chat. It is a sanity check, not a bid: real proposals still go through the Proposal Engine.

## Finding the estimate or proposal

Each opportunity gathers its related work on its own page — so the estimate, documents, and links live with the deal, not in a separate folder or an email thread.

1. Open the opportunity.
2. Scroll its page to the related sections (estimates, documents, links).
3. Open what you need right there — every generated file also leaves a timeline note saying who generated what and when.

## What good looks like

The bar: every deal you own with pricing in flight shows the correct proposal stage and due date in Estimation & Proposals, has its estimate attached to the record, and flips to Pricing Complete the moment the one-pager exists. A teammate should be able to pick up your deal cold and find every number and document from the opportunity page alone.

Open the **Estimation & Proposals** view, pick a deal with an upcoming due date, and confirm its proposal stage is current and its estimate is attached.
