## Stage vs. bid status — two different things

Every opportunity carries two trackers, and they answer different questions:

- **Stage** — where the deal is in your *sales process* (early lead → screening → proposal → negotiation → won/lost).
- **Bid status** — the *RFP outcome* specifically: received, submitted, shortlisted, won, lost, no-bid.

Together they give a complete read — a deal can be mid-process *and* already submitted on the bid side. Keep both current.

## Moving a deal forward

1. Open the opportunity.
2. Update the **Stage** as it progresses.
3. Update **bid status** as the RFP side moves.

**Or just ask:** *"Move the Suns scoreboard deal to proposal stage."*

## Marking won, lost, or no-bid

When a deal lands — or doesn't — set it straight away:

- **Won** → set bid status to **Won** and fill in the close details.
- **Lost** or **No-bid** → set the status so the pipeline stays honest and win/loss reporting is accurate.

It takes a second and it keeps everyone's forecast real. A pipeline full of stale "open" deals helps no one.

**Or just ask:** *"Mark the Marlins ribbon board as no-bid."*

## Probability

Probability is a rough read on how likely a deal is to land. It weights the forecast and lets leadership filter to the deals most likely to close. Nudge it up or down as your confidence changes.

## Handing a deal off

Update **who's responsible** on the opportunity and leave a **note** with the context. Because everything lives on the record, the next person walks in with the full picture — nothing gets lost in a hallway conversation or a buried email.

<Callout title="What happens after we win">
Winning is marked on the opportunity, and the **account** is the connective tissue — so the service team can see the won work tied to that company. The account carries the relationship from sale into service automatically.
</Callout>
