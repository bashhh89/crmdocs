## Stage vs. bid status — two different things

Every opportunity carries two trackers, and they answer different questions:

- **Stage** — where the deal is in your *sales process*: New → Screening → Meeting → Proposal → Customer.
- <GlossaryTerm term="bidStatus" /> — the *bid outcome* specifically: RFP Received → Scoping → Bid Submitted → Shortlisted → Won / Lost / No Bid.

Together they give a complete read — a deal can be mid-process *and* already submitted on the bid side. In day-to-day practice, the team reads **bid status** first; it is what the Bid Tracker board and the win/loss reporting run on. But keep both current: stage tells the relationship story, bid status tells the bid story, and different people rely on each.

## Moving a deal forward

1. Open the opportunity.
2. Update the **Stage** as it progresses.
3. Update **bid status** as the RFP side moves.
4. Leave a one-line note when something meaningful changed — the next person to open the deal should not have to call you.

Note that one transition happens automatically: when a proposal PDF is generated in the Proposal Engine, the deal's bid status moves to **Bid Submitted** on its own, and the PDF attaches to the deal. That is <GlossaryTerm term="universalCrmPush" /> recording real work — do not "correct" it backward.

## Marking won, lost, or no-bid

When a deal lands — or doesn't — set it straight away:

- **Won** → set bid status to **Won** and fill in the close details, including the **Completion / Contract End Date**. The platform enforces this one: a win without a completion date gets reverted and you get a notification explaining why. Fill in the date, then set Won again.
- **Lost** or **No Bid** → set the status the day you know, so the pipeline stays honest and win/loss reporting is accurate. If you know why you lost, put it in a note — that is the raw material for every future win-rate conversation.

It takes a second and it keeps everyone's forecast real. A pipeline full of stale "open" deals helps no one — and it quietly inflates the number leadership plans against.

## Probability

Probability is a rough read on how likely a deal is to land, on a 0–100 scale. It weights the forecast and lets leadership filter to the deals most likely to close. Nudge it up or down as your confidence changes, and leave the reason in a note — "probability moved to 70 after the site visit went well" is worth more than the number alone. The discipline is honesty, not optimism: an inflated probability does not make a deal more likely to close, it just makes the forecast wrong in your name.

## Handing a deal off

Update **who's responsible** on the opportunity and leave a **note** with the context: where the deal stands, what was promised, what the next step is and when. Because everything lives on the record — documents, estimates, timeline, contacts — the next person walks in with the full picture. Nothing gets lost in a hallway conversation or a buried email. A good handoff note takes ninety seconds and saves the receiver a week of reconstruction.

<Callout title="What happens after we win">
Winning is marked on the opportunity, and an automatic win notification goes out to the team — so a properly-marked win announces itself. The **account** is the connective tissue: the service team sees the won work tied to that Company, and the relationship carries from sale into service automatically. This is also why wins must be marked promptly — the downstream teams are literally waiting on the signal.
</Callout>
