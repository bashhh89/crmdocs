Start with the work in front of you. Then jump to the training that matches it.

Three tools carry the whole operation, and each track teaches one of them plus the seams between them. The **CRM** holds Companies, opportunities, and the pipeline — it is where deals live and where every other system reports back to. The **Proposal Engine** is where RFPs get analyzed, priced, and packaged into one-pagers and SOWs — and everything it generates attaches itself to the right CRM deal automatically. The **Service Dashboard** runs the field — tickets, events, technicians, venue hardware — and mirrors into the CRM in about a second. You do not need to learn all three. You need to learn yours, plus where your work hands off to the next person's.

### Q2: Are you trying to do work or read work?

- **Do work:** pick Sales / Technology, Services, Media & Sponsorship, or Proposal Engine.
- **Read work:** pick Leadership or dashboards.
- **Clean work:** pick Working Standard first.

### Q3: Is the answer client-facing?

- **Yes:** verify the source records before sending.
- **No:** use the assistant for speed, then clean the record if you find a gap.

The distinction matters because the cost of being wrong is different. An internal number that is slightly off costs a correction in a meeting. A client-facing number that is off costs trust. When the output leaves the building — a proposal figure, a service status, a renewal quote — open the records behind it first. When it stays internal, move fast and fix the data as you go.

<Callout title="Where this lives">

### Services Scenario

It is game week. A venue has an open display issue, a scheduled event, and a client asking whether the site is ready. You need the Company, tickets, event activity, and recent service notes in one place.

## Before / After

The goal of training is not more clicks. It is cleaner work.

<Shot src="/img/screenshots/crm/t-all-deals.png" caption="Before: a list can show many open records, but it is only useful if the rows have current owners, dates, stages, values, and next steps." />

<Shot src="/img/screenshots/crm/t-account.png" caption="After: a clean Company view gives the next person the relationship, opportunities, contacts, notes, and service history in one place." />

That contrast is the whole point of every track on this page. The tools are already connected — proposals attach themselves, service work syncs itself, dashboards read live. What training adds is the human half: records created in the right place, linked to the right Company, updated when the state changes. Ten clean records beat a hundred stale ones, because clean records are the only ones anyone downstream can act on.
