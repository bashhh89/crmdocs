## What You Are Trying To Do

This track is for service contracts, maintenance, field operations, and venue support.

You are learning how to:

1. **Track what's broken** - create, assign, escalate, and close tickets.
2. **See the full venue story** - Company, contacts, contracts, opportunities, tickets, notes, and history.
3. **Check venue health** - recent activity, recurring service, event context, and outstanding issues.
4. **Keep the handoff clean** - make sure the next person can see status without hunting.

None of these are new skills — you already do this work. What changes is *where the record lives*. When status lives on the venue instead of in your inbox, a colleague can cover your venue mid-shift, leadership can answer a client without pinging you, and nothing depends on you remembering it.

## Two surfaces, one story

You work across two connected surfaces, and knowing which one is "home" for each kind of work is half the track:

- **The Service Dashboard** is the working surface — tickets, events, staffing, game-day workflows, and field operations live here. When something breaks, gets scheduled, or gets fixed, this is where it happens.
- **The CRM** is the relationship surface — the Company record, contacts, deals, contract scope, and the full account history live here. This is where you read a venue before a call or a visit.

The bridge between them is <GlossaryTerm term="realTimeCrmSync" />. When a ticket is opened, updated, or closed in the Service Dashboard — or an event is scheduled, or a technician moves through the game-day workflow — the change appears on the CRM Company page in about a second. You never copy anything across, and you never wonder which version is current. Work where the work lives; read where the relationship lives.

## Day 1 / Day 30 / Reference

- **Day 1:** open the Company, find open tickets, check event context, and read the latest note.
- **Day 30:** spot venue health patterns, overdue issues, repeated failures, and handoff gaps.
- **Reference as needed:** <GlossaryTerm term="iotOperationsWorkspace" />, walkthroughs, maintenance records, reports, portals.

The Day 30 skill is the one that matters. Anyone can read one ticket. The operator-level skill is reading a *venue*: the same display failing three times in two months, a ticket that has sat unowned since last week, an event on Saturday with an unresolved issue in front of it. That pattern reading is what this track builds toward.

Take them in order the first time — tickets are the raw material, the Company view is where they become a story, activity is how the story stays current, and the day-to-day lesson turns all of it into a habit. After that, each page stands alone as a reference.

## The quality bar

You are done with this track when, for any venue you cover, you can answer four questions in under a minute without asking anyone:

1. What is open right now, and who owns each item?
2. What is happening at the venue this week?
3. What happened the last time we were on site?
4. What did ANC sell this account, and who are the people we deal with?

If any answer requires digging through email or memory, the record is not current — and fixing that is the job.
