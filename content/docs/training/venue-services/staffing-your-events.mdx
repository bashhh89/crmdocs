**After this lesson** you'll be able to staff a week of events in one pass, decide what belongs on your venue's schedule, and tell at a glance which of tonight's events are actually covered.

Staffing is the part of this job that is invisible when it works. Nobody thanks you for a fully covered Saturday; everybody notices the one event that went out unassigned. This lesson is the routine that makes the second thing not happen.

## What lands on your schedule, and what waits for you

Home sporting events for your venue's teams go onto the master schedule automatically. Anything else the venue calendar carries — concerts, private hires, one-off bookings — does **not**. It waits for you under **Suggested**.

<Callout type="info">
You are notified when suggestions arrive for a venue you lead. Approving puts the event on the master schedule exactly as if it had always been there — you can then staff it normally. Rejecting removes it for good, so the same concert will not reappear every time the calendar syncs.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn">
Only the lead for that venue can make this call. If you cannot see the buttons on a suggestion, that venue is led by someone else — which is deliberate. It stops anyone quietly adding work to a building they do not run.
</Callout>

## Staffing an event

1. Open **Events** and filter to the week ahead.
2. Sort or filter to **Needs Staffing** — these are the events with nobody on them.
3. Open an event and assign the tech or techs covering it.
4. Check the assignment landed — the event should now show the names.

<WhereThisLives tool="services" path="Service Dashboard → Events → Needs Staffing → open the event → assign" />

<Callout type="warn">
Not every event needs staff. Warranty-only venues are covered without an assignment, and those events are marked as such — do not chase names for them. Chasing coverage on events that never needed it is the fastest way to stop trusting the unassigned count.
</Callout>

## Watching the workflow land

Once an event is staffed, you get told as each step happens: the tech checks in, marks game ready, and files the post-event report. The post-event notification tells you straight away whether an incident was reported.

You are still alerted when a step is **missed** — that has not changed. What is new is that you also see the steps that go right, so a quiet night reads as confirmed rather than unknown.
