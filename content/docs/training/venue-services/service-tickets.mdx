**After this lesson** you'll be able to raise a ticket, assign it, track it to done, and escalate when needed — with everything tied to the right venue.

A ticket is not paperwork. It is the one place where an issue, its owner, its urgency, and its resolution live together — visible to the field, to leadership, and to the client-facing side of the business at the same time. A venue with clean tickets is a venue anyone can cover. A venue with issues living in text threads is a venue only one person can cover, and that person is always you.

## How tickets work here

Service tickets are **tied back to their account** — so when you open a venue, you see what's been raised, what's open, and what's handled. You're not hunting across systems to figure out the state of a venue.

Every ticket carries the same core anatomy: a title and description, a **priority** (low, medium, high, critical), a **status** that moves from new through in progress to closed, a **category** (hardware, software, content, operational), the **venue** it belongs to, and an **assigned owner**. Priority is not decoration — it drives the response and resolution deadlines the team is measured against, so set it to match reality: a dark board with a game in three days is not "low."

Tickets also arrive on their own. A client emailing the support address, calling the support line and leaving a voicemail, or submitting from their portal page all become tickets automatically — they land in the same list and behave the same way as one you create by hand. Your job on those is triage: confirm the venue is right, set the priority, and get an owner on it.

## Create a ticket

1. Make a **new ticket**.
2. Link it to the **venue's account** — that's what keeps everything for the venue together.
3. Add the issue and enough detail that someone else could act on it: what is affected, what the client reported, and any deadline pressure (an event date changes everything).

<PromptCard accent="services" title="Create a ticket by asking" prompt="Open a ticket for TD Garden about the ribbon board." />

## Assign it

Set the **owner** on the ticket so it's clear who's handling it. Nothing sits unowned, and anyone looking at the venue can see who's on it. Some tickets get auto-assigned by rule when they arrive; anything the rules don't catch lands in the **unassigned** queue — that queue should be empty by the end of every triage pass.

## Track its status

Each ticket has a **status** as it moves from open to resolved. Move it as the work actually progresses — status is how the rest of the business reads the venue without calling you. Because of <GlossaryTerm term="realTimeCrmSync" />, every status change you make shows up on the CRM Company page in about a second.

<PromptCard accent="services" title="Check a venue’s open work" prompt="What’s still open at TD Garden?" />

## See overdue or aging work

Filter your venue view to what's open and sort by age — the open items stay visible instead of slipping. An old open ticket is one of two things: work that stalled, or work that finished without the record being closed. Both need the same fix — open it and make the record match reality.

<PromptCard accent="services" title="Find what’s slipping" prompt="What’s overdue across my venues?" />

## Escalate

Raise it on the ticket and assign or note it to the right person, so the escalation is **on the record** — not just a hallway conversation. Everyone looking at the venue sees it's been escalated, and if the issue resurfaces in a client conversation, the timeline is already written.

## Close it properly

Closing is where most ticket hygiene dies. A closed ticket with no resolution note is a hole in the venue's history — six months later, when the same display fails again, "what did we do last time?" has no answer. Before you close: state what the fix was, in one or two lines. That sentence is the cheapest documentation you will ever write.

<Callout title="Ticket not showing on the Company page?">
The sync matches by venue. If a ticket's venue doesn't line up with the CRM venue record, its tickets can stop appearing on the Company page. Don't work around it — flag it, and an admin fixes the match on the CRM venue record once. After that, everything flows.
</Callout>
