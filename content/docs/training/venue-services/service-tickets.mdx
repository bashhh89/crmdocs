## How tickets work here

Service tickets are **tied back to their account** — so when you open a venue, you see what's been raised, what's open, and what's handled. You're not hunting across systems to figure out the state of a venue.

## Create a ticket

1. Make a **new ticket**.
2. Link it to the **venue's account** — that's what keeps everything for the venue together.
3. Add the issue and any detail.

**Or just ask:** *"Open a ticket for TD Garden about the ribbon board."*

## Assign it

Set the **owner** on the ticket so it's clear who's handling it. Nothing sits unowned, and anyone looking at the venue can see who's on it.

## Track its status

Each ticket has a **status** as it moves from open to resolved. Filter your view to **open** to see what's outstanding.

**Or just ask:** *"What's still open at TD Garden?"*

## See overdue or aging work

Filter your venue view to what's open and sort by age — the open items stay visible instead of slipping.

**Or just ask:** *"What's overdue across my venues?"*

## Escalate

Raise it on the ticket and assign or note it to the right person, so the escalation is **on the record** — not just a hallway conversation. Everyone looking at the venue sees it's been escalated.

## Now you try →

Open a venue, create a test ticket linked to it, assign it to yourself, then filter your view to "open."
