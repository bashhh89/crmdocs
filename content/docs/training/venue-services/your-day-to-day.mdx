This is the lesson that turns the other three into a system. Knowing how tickets work, how the account view reads, and how activity gets logged is capability. ## See all your venues in one list

Set up a view filtered to **your venues** and make it your default landing spot. One glance tells you where things stand across all of them.

1. Open **Companies** (or your venues list).
2. Add a filter for the venues you cover.
3. Save it as your own view and **star** it.

<WhereThisLives tool="crm" path="CRM → Companies → filter to your venues → save + star the view" />

<PromptCard accent="services" title="Your venues, one line" prompt="Show me my venues." />

You build this once. From then on, "where do things stand?" is a single click, every day, on data that is already live — tickets and events flow in from the Service Dashboard through <GlossaryTerm term="realTimeCrmSync" />, so the view is current the moment you open it.

## The daily habit

Because everything for a venue ties to its account, open items stay visible instead of getting lost in email. The habit that keeps it clean:

- **Each morning:** check what's open or overdue across your venues.
- **As you work:** keep the venue you touched current — a quick note or status update.

<PromptCard accent="services" title="The morning question" prompt="What’s open or overdue across my venues?" />

When the morning pass surfaces something, triage it on the spot instead of leaving it for "later":

<Callout title="The whole point">
Nothing falls through the cracks because nothing lives in your inbox anymore — it lives on the account, where you and the whole team can see it. Keep the accounts current and the system does the remembering for you.
</Callout>

## The weekly pass

Once a week, zoom out from the daily check and read your venues the way the [account view lesson](/docs/training/venue-services/the-account-view) taught: not "what is open today" but "how is each venue trending." Repeat failures, recurring service coming due, thin activity trails, upcoming event clusters. Ten minutes, once a week, and you stop being surprised by your own venues.

## Common mistakes

<OhShit
  symptom="You skipped the morning check for a busy week, and a client escalated a ticket you never saw — it arrived automatically from a voicemail and sat unowned."
  fix="Own the miss, assign it, resolve it, and reply with status and next step. Then run the full pass — where one item slipped, others usually did too."
  prevention="The check is 30 seconds precisely so busy weeks cannot kill it. Chain it to something you already do every morning — first coffee, first sit-down — so it survives game weeks."
/>

The subtler failure: doing the check but not acting on it. Reading "unowned ticket, 4 days old" and moving on is worse than not looking — you had the information and left the risk standing. The pass is check *and* touch: every red flag gets an owner, a nudge, or an escalation before you close the view.

## You only learn your part

You don't need the sales or media workflows — just Venue Services. Everyone does the same Core basics, then only the track that fits their work. Your daily view, your venues, your queue.

## What good looks like

The habit is working when three things are true. Your morning check genuinely takes under a minute most days, because the previous days left nothing to clean up. Nobody has to ask you for status, because the record already says it. And when you take a day off, the venue does not go dark — anyone can open your view and see exactly where everything stands. That last one is the real test: the system remembers, so no single person has to.
