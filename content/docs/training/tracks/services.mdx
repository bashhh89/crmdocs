Triage tickets, staff events, use <GlossaryTerm term="iotOperationsWorkspace" />, and log walkthroughs. The Service Dashboard is where the field actually runs — tickets, events, technicians, venue hardware. The CRM reads from it, about a second behind, so leadership and sales always see live operational truth without ever pinging the ops team. Your rule of thumb: do the work in the Service Dashboard, let the CRM catch up on its own.

## Scenario 1: The Flickering Display

<ToolBadge tool="services" />

> *Section 110, display 3 is flickering. Two hours to tip-off. The client just called it in.*

Log the ticket in Service Dashboard.

Fields to set:

- venue = venue name
- priority = HIGH for game-day issues
- description = Display 3 section 110 flickering
- category = HARDWARE

**Why each field matters:** priority is not decoration — it drives the response and resolution deadlines on the ticket, so a high-priority game-day issue gets a tighter clock than a Tuesday cosmetic fix. Category routes the ticket: when an auto-assignment rule matches the category and venue, the ticket lands on the right technician without a dispatcher touching it. If no rule matches, it goes to the Unassigned queue — which someone must be watching on game day.

Write the description for the technician who has not seen the display: what is wrong, where exactly, since when, and what the client already tried. "Board broken" costs a phone call; "Display 3, section 110, flickering intermittently since doors, power-cycled once by venue staff" does not.

**What happens automatically:** the ticket appears on the CRM Company record in about a second through <GlossaryTerm term="realTimeCrmSync" />. Every later change — status, assignment, resolution — syncs the same way. Sales sees the full service history before the next renewal conversation, and nobody ever forwards a ticket screenshot to leadership.

<PromptCard accent="services" title="Check the open load on a venue" prompt="Show open tickets for this venue sorted by priority, with assignee and status." />

<WhereThisLives tool="services" path="Service Dashboard -> Tickets -> New Ticket" />

**Common mistake:** working the issue and logging the ticket afterward. If it is not in the system, there is no deadline clock, no assignment, and no record on the account when someone asks in June what happened in February. Log first, fix second.

## Scenario 2: IoT Operations Workspace

<ToolBadge tool="services" />

> *You need the IP address for the main rack and the walkthrough log from last month.*

Open Service Dashboard -> Venues -> venue -> <GlossaryTerm term="iotOperationsWorkspace" />.

What is there:

- Displays grid — installed displays, physical locations, model details, status
- Rack/device notes with IP addresses
- Walkthrough logs — inspection visits, results, follow-up status
- Maintenance schedule rows — affected equipment, assigned techs, resolution notes
- Forms and operational documents

**Which surface for which job:** the Operations Workspace is for table-first operational records — what hardware exists, where it is, what state it is in, what the field team observed. Tickets are for support cases with a deadline, a client, and a resolution. Venue settings are for the venue record itself. If you are logging a walkthrough, that is the workspace. If the walkthrough found a dead display that needs fixing this week, that finding becomes a ticket too — the walkthrough row is the observation, the ticket is the action.

**CRM link:** this data can surface on the Company record, but editing happens in the Service Dashboard. Never try to fix an operational record from the CRM side — the dashboard is the source of truth and the CRM is the mirror.

**What good looks like:** every venue visit leaves a walkthrough row the same day, with follow-up status set. Six months of consistent walkthrough logs is what turns "I think that rack has been flaky" into a documented pattern the client will pay to fix.

## Scenario 3: Event Staffing Workflow

<ToolBadge tool="services" />

> *Tonight's game needs a technician. You need to assign and track the workflow.*

Open Service Dashboard -> Events -> event -> Assign Technician.

The workflow:

1. Check-in: tech arrives on-site.
2. Game-ready: equipment checked, boards operational, content loaded.
3. Post-game report: event ended, issues logged, equipment status recorded.

The platform runs the follow-up for you. Assigned techs get an automatic reminder hours before the event; if the tech has not checked in as the event approaches, the reminders escalate to managers, and a missing check-in at start time raises a critical alert. You do not need to babysit the board — you need to react when an escalation actually fires.

The post-game report matters more than it looks. It stays editable for roughly a day after submission, then locks and becomes the canonical record of the event. If a display failed during the third quarter and the report does not say so, that failure never happened as far as the account history is concerned.
