**After this lesson** you'll be able to find duplicate and empty accounts, merge the duplicates without losing anything, and know the one move to never make.

Why this lesson exists: years of venue business — migrated history from the legacy CRM, imports, and hand-entered records — leave any account base with look-alikes. The same organization can appear as the team, the venue, and the ownership group, each holding a slice of the real history. That split history has teeth: a bid team pulling references sees half the past projects, a dashboard undercounts an account's value, and two salespeople work the "same" client without knowing it. Cleanup is how the account layer stays worth trusting.

## The golden rule

There are two jobs here, and keeping them separate is the whole game:

- **The AI finds.** It's brilliant at spotting duplicates and empty accounts across thousands of records in seconds.
- **You decide.** Merging is permanent-feeling and touches real data, so a person reviews each one and confirms it.

<Callout type="warn">
Never ask the AI to merge or "condense" accounts for you in one go. Finding is safe; merging in bulk on a single command is how good records get damaged. The AI hands you the list — you do the merges.
</Callout>

## Step 1 — Find the duplicates

Open the assistant and ask in plain English. Good prompts to copy:

<PromptCard accent="crm" title="Duplicate sweep" prompt="List companies that look like duplicates of each other — same team or venue, slightly different names." />

<PromptCard accent="crm" title="One family of look-alikes" prompt="Show me every account named something like Red Sox so I can see the duplicates." />

<PromptCard accent="crm" title="Find the empties" prompt="Which accounts have no opportunities, no contacts, and no tickets?" />

<PromptCard accent="crm" title="Find the junk names" prompt="Find accounts whose names start with OLD or TEST or are blank." />

The AI gives you a clean list. That list is your worklist — work it top to bottom.

Not everything that looks like a duplicate is one. A team and its venue can legitimately be **separate** accounts (different organizations, different contacts, different contracts). The tell for a true duplicate is the same organization with overlapping history — same contacts appearing on both, deals that obviously belong to one relationship. When in doubt, open both and read before merging.

## Step 2 — Merge a duplicate, one at a time

This uses the CRM's built-in **Merge** — it keeps one record and folds everything from the other into it, so no opportunities, contacts, or tickets are lost.

1. Go to **Companies** and find the two (or more) duplicates from your list.
2. **Tick the checkbox** on the left of each duplicate row so they're all selected.
3. At the **top-right**, open the **⋮ (three-dots) menu** — choose **Merge**.
4. The CRM shows the records side by side. **Pick the keeper** — the most complete, correctly-named one — and confirm.
5. The keeper now holds all the opportunities, people, and tickets from both. The duplicate is removed.

Choosing the keeper: prefer the record with the correct official name, the most complete fields (owner, league, city), and the deeper history. Everything from the other record moves over anyway — you're choosing which name and field set survives.

Then move to the next pair on your list. One at a time is the right speed — it's how you stay sure.

## Step 3 — Clear out the empty accounts

For accounts the AI flagged as truly empty (no opportunities, no people, no tickets, no notes):

1. Open the account and **confirm with your own eyes** that it's genuinely empty.
2. If it is, delete it from the record's actions menu. Deletes are recoverable for a window, so a mistake isn't fatal — but check first anyway.

An account that is empty **today** isn't automatically junk — it might be a prospect someone just created. If the name looks intentional and recent, ask around before deleting. The confident deletes are the obvious ones: test entries, blanks, and long-dead imports.

## How to be sure nothing was lost

After a merge, open the keeper and glance at its tabs:

- **Opportunities** — the counts from both records should now both be there.
- **People** and **Tickets** — same.
- If a number looks short, tell the assistant *"show me the opportunities on this account"* and compare against what you expected.

A 10-second check after each merge is all it takes to work with total confidence.

## The one rule you never break

<Callout type="warn">
Use the AI to **find**, never to **merge in bulk**. "Condense all the duplicates" is the one command that can quietly damage real records. You stay in control of every merge — that's what keeps the CRM clean *and* safe.
</Callout>

## What good looks like

A finished cleanup pass means: the assistant's duplicate list is worked to zero or parked with reasons; every merge got its 10-second count check; empty-account deletes were confirmed visually first; and no bulk merge was ever run. The account layer now reads one organization = one record — which is exactly what [bid reference pulls](/docs/training/core/account-info-for-bids) and account-level reporting depend on.

## Now you try →

Ask the assistant for one set of look-alike accounts, then merge a single pair using the steps above — and do the 10-second check after. That's the whole loop. Once it feels natural, you can work down a full list.
