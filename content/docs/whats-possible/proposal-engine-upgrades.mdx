# Proposal Engine Upgrades

The Proposal Engine already pushes everything it generates into the CRM. The next set of upgrades flip the relationship the other way — making the Proposal Engine *aware* of what's happening in the CRM and Slack, and acting on it.

---

## 1. Inbound RFP email → deal, automatically

> **The benefit** — RFPs land in the system the moment they arrive. No "this came in three days ago and nobody saw it."
>
> **What we already do** — every artifact Natalia generates (proposal, SOW, Excel, one-pager) auto-attaches to the right deal in the CRM, no manual upload.
>
> **What changes** — when an RFP comes in by email, the system auto-creates the Company (if needed), creates the deal, attaches the email and any PDFs, and routes it to the right estimator based on rules.
>
> **Rough effort** — about a week.

The pattern is identical to the proposal-out flow we already have, just running in reverse. Today the proposal goes out → CRM. Tomorrow the RFP comes in → CRM, with the same automation that catches every artifact and lands it on the right Account.

---

## 2. Stuck-deal nudges in Slack

> **The benefit** — pipeline never goes stale. Deals don't quietly sit at "BAFO" for a month.
>
> **What we already do** — the Service Dashboard already fires Slack reminders for missed game-ready check-ins.
>
> **What changes** — the same reminder engine watches deals. If a deal sits at BAFO or Shortlisted for more than 14 days, the assigned account exec gets a Slack ping with a drafted follow-up email ready to send.
>
> **Rough effort** — about three days.

The reminder pattern is proven (the Service Dashboard does it for tens of events a week). Pointing it at deal stages instead of workflow stages is the smallest possible change.

---

## 3. AI win/loss learnings on every closed deal

> **The benefit** — institutional memory that doesn't walk out the door when someone leaves.
>
> **What we already do** — the RFP Analyzer reads documents and extracts structure. The Copilot already explains pricing reasoning.
>
> **What changes** — when a deal closes (won or lost), AI drafts a one-paragraph "what happened" note, attached to the deal. Tags themes like *"lost on price"*, *"won on relationship"*, *"won despite slower delivery"*. Over time, these tags surface trends.
>
> **Rough effort** — about a week.

Once a year of deals are tagged this way, you can ask: *"Of the deals we lost in 2026, what fraction were on price?"* — and get a real answer. Sales coaching becomes data, not gut feel.

---

## 4. Auto-suggested pricing from past deals

> **The benefit** — first-pass pricing in seconds, with confidence ranges based on real history.
>
> **What we already do** — the AI Quick estimator generates first-pass line items from a natural-language brief. The CRM has 8,360 historical deals with revenue, margin, and bid status.
>
> **What changes** — when a new RFP arrives, AI looks at similar past deals (same league, similar size, same scope) and suggests a price band: *"deals like this won at $1.2M – $1.5M; lost above $1.6M."*
>
> **Rough effort** — about two weeks.

This turns the estimator from a calculator into a coach. Estimators still make the call — but with a starting point that reflects what the market actually paid for similar work.

---

## 5. Probability prediction on every deal

> **The benefit** — better forecasts. The dashboard's "qualified pipeline" number actually means something.
>
> **What we already do** — every deal carries probability today (manually set, often stale). 8,360 historical deals = enough training data.
>
> **What changes** — AI looks at deal attributes (amount, league, account history, stage age, who the AE is) and predicts win probability automatically. Updates daily.
>
> **Rough effort** — about two weeks for a working model. Ongoing tuning.

The current 50% probability filter on Jireh's dashboard is a manual judgment. Replace the manual judgment with a model trained on your own deal history and the forecast becomes meaningful.

---

## How these stack

Each upgrade is independent. Pick the one with the highest pain. But notice the compounding effect:

- (1) gets RFPs into the system faster
- (2) makes sure they don't go stale
- (3) builds the data that feeds (4) and (5)
- (4) and (5) make pricing and forecasting more accurate

Every step makes the next one cheaper to build, because the data is already there.
