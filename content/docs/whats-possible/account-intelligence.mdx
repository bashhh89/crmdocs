# Account Intelligence

The CRM is already where everything lands — proposals, tickets, events, revenue, design requests. The next set of upgrades use that combined data to give leadership and AEs a *single picture* of every account.

---

## 1. Account 360 — one screen, the whole story

> **The benefit** — leadership can answer any question about an account in 30 seconds, without hunting across tabs.
>
> **What we already do** — every Company page in the CRM has tabs for Opportunities, Tickets, Events, Revenue Splits, Estimates, Design Requests. Already account-centric.
>
> **What changes** — a single landing pane on every Company that combines:
>
> - **Financial** — total revenue, total margin, paid %, year-over-year trend
> - **Operational** — open tickets, SLA breach rate, workflow completion
> - **Sales** — open deals, weighted pipeline, last activity date
> - **Health** — composite score (see [Customer Health](./service-dashboard-upgrades))
>
> Plus a *"what changed this week"* timeline.
>
> **Rough effort** — about two weeks.

This is the demo Jireh asked for verbatim: *"an account management tool is what we're really looking for."* Every datapoint already exists. The work is bringing them onto one pane.

---

## 2. Monday-morning AI briefing per executive

> **The benefit** — leadership starts the week informed without 15 minutes of clicking.
>
> **What we already do** — Scout already answers *"what should I look at today?"* on demand.
>
> **What changes** — every Monday at 8 a.m., each executive gets a personalized one-page briefing in their inbox or Slack:
>
> - **Jireh** — pipeline movement, top deals, accounts trending down
> - **Joe** — venues with rising tickets, missed workflows, staffing gaps
> - **Charlie** — payment status, billable hours, contract expirations
> - **Natalia** — RFPs due this week, deals in BAFO, pricing-complete queue
>
> Each briefing is generated by AI from the live CRM data — no human prep work.
>
> **Rough effort** — about a week.

Already-running pieces: Scout answering questions, Slack as delivery, the data being there. New piece: a scheduled job that asks Scout the same six questions every Monday and formats the answer.

---

## 3. Auto-tagged learnings on every closed deal

> **The benefit** — sales coaching becomes data-driven, not memory-driven.
>
> **What we already do** — every closed deal has a status (Won / Lost / No Bid), an amount, a league, and a timeline of events.
>
> **What changes** — when a deal closes, AI tags it with up to three themes from a fixed taxonomy:
>
> - *Won on relationship* / *Won on price* / *Won on speed* / *Won on incumbent*
> - *Lost on price* / *Lost on timing* / *Lost on scope mismatch* / *Lost to incumbent*
> - *No-bid: capacity* / *No-bid: strategic mismatch*
>
> Six months in, you can ask: *"What % of NFL losses in 2026 were on price?"* and get a real answer.
>
> **Rough effort** — about a week. Then ongoing tag refinement.

This is the kind of question Jireh might want answered for the board. Today it requires a human to read 50 deal records and judge. After this, it's a query.

---

## 4. Contract value at risk — the at-a-glance number

> **The benefit** — leadership knows in real time how much committed revenue is at risk.
>
> **What we already do** — every account has a customer health score (in the upgrade above). Every contract has end dates and paid status.
>
> **What changes** — a single number on the executive dashboard: *"Contract value at risk in next 90 days = $12.3M"* — calculated from accounts with low health scores or expiring contracts.
>
> **Rough effort** — about three days once health scores exist.

A real number that maps to a real risk. Today the closest equivalent is the unweighted pipeline forecast. This is the *defensive* number — what you might lose if you do nothing.

---

## 5. Year-over-year account trends

> **The benefit** — instantly see which accounts are growing, shrinking, or flat.
>
> **What we already do** — revenue is tracked per fiscal year on every deal. The CRM has 5 years of forward visibility (2026 through 2030).
>
> **What changes** — every Company page gets a trend line: revenue this year vs. last year, with the delta highlighted. Sort the account list by *largest decrease* to find accounts at risk.
>
> **Rough effort** — about a week.

The data is there. This is mostly about visualization plus a sort key on the account list.

---

## How these stack

These four upgrades work as a unit. Build them in order:

- (1) Account 360 gives you the surface.
- (2) Monday briefing gives you the regular cadence.
- (3) Auto-tagging gives you the analytical depth.
- (4) Contract-at-risk and (5) trend lines feed off the data the others produce.

The combined effect: leadership can run the business by glancing at the CRM once a week, instead of pulling reports on demand.
