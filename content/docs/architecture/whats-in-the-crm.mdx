## The CRM has two hubs

Most CRMs have one hub: the account. ANC's has two: the **Company** (the commercial side — who you're selling to, who's paying) and the **Venue** (the physical side — the building, the screens, the events, the on-site work).

Every other record in the system traces back to one of these two hubs — most of the time, both.

Look at the Venue object: 18 different objects link back to it.

| Sales side | Operations side | Physical infrastructure |
|---|---|---|
| Deals | Service Tickets | Stations |
| Estimates | Tasks | Racks |
| RFP Analyses | Walkthrough Logs (15,465 records) | Rack Devices |
| | Events | Display Locations |
| | Staff Members | Parts Orders |
| | Opening Checklist Items | Services |

That's why the meeting demo flows so naturally. Open a venue → see every deal sold there, every ticket open at it, every walkthrough log against it, every staff member who works it, every screen installed in it. One screen, the whole story.

The 15,465 Walkthrough Logs alone are a multi-year record of every on-site visit — date, time, technician, locations visited, issues found. That's *real* operational depth, not vapor.

## Records are connected, not isolated

Beyond the two hubs, every record links to a tight web of related records. Three concrete examples:

### Revenue Splits — 5,160 records, every one tied to a Deal

Revenue Splits exist so a multi-year deal can be broken down per fiscal year. Every single one of the 5,160 records is connected to its parent Deal. Open a deal → see its splits. Open the dashboard → the same splits drive the per-year revenue widgets.

This is the cross-object plumbing working at scale, in production, today.

### Service Tickets — 3,901 records, connected to 6 different things

A Service Ticket isn't just a row in a list. Every one of the 3,901 tickets in the CRM is linked to up to **six** other objects:

- **Account (Company)** — the client the ticket is about
- **Deal** — the deal that brought the venue under contract
- **Venue** — the physical venue where the issue happened
- **Inventory Asset** — the specific hardware (display, processor, rack)
- **Person** — the contact who reported it
- **Event** — the game or event the ticket is tied to

Plus a **"Services Dashboard ID"** field — the key that makes the real-time sync from the Service Dashboard work. When ops opens a ticket on their side, that ID gets written here, and every subsequent update flows through automatically.

That's why opening a Company in the CRM shows you everything happening with the account in one screen — the relations carry the operational story onto the account view automatically.

### RFP Analyses — 23 fields, 3 entity relations, 0 records yet

RFP Analyses is the most interesting empty object. The schema is complete and rich:

**Fields** (23 total) — Filename, Project Name, Client Name, Location, Venue, Budget Range, Project Timeline, Total LED Sq Ft, Page Count, Key Requirements, Extracted Specs (JSON), Specs Found, Relevant Pages, Source, Status, Notes, Proposal Engine ID, plus standard tracking.

**Relations** — links to Account (Company), Deal, and Venue.

Translation: every RFP that arrives could land in the CRM with the deal, the venue, the client, the budget, the requirements, and the extracted specs already populated — all on the right Company page.

What's missing today is one bridge: pushing the Proposal Engine's RFP Analyzer output into this object. Once that's wired (about a week of work), every RFP analysis Natalia runs lands in the CRM automatically — exactly the same pattern as the proposal artifacts that already auto-land. See the [Inbound RFP capture pitch](/docs/whats-possible/proposal-engine-upgrades) for the upgrade.

## What the empty objects mean

A handful of objects have zero records today. Most are **infrastructure built in advance**, ready to populate as the next phase of work lands:

- **Ad Campaigns / Ad Channels / Ad Deliverables** — the advertising / sponsorship side. Schema ready; data starts flowing when sponsorship reporting lights up.
- **Estimates / Estimate Lines** — wired for Scout's Quick Estimate skill. Each one Scout produces creates rows here.
- **Manufacturers / Rate Cards** — upstream of LED Products; the products carry the data today.
- **Print Requests / RMA Trackers / Parts** — operational pipelines staged for when the warehouse system connects.
- **RFP Analyses** — see above. Schema complete with 23 fields + 3 relations. Awaiting the Proposal Engine bridge.
- **Services** — the **recurring service contracts** object. Fully modeled with Monthly Value, Start Date, End Date, Service Type, Status, "Requires Staffing" flag, and links to Account + Venue. 0 records today. Once contract data flows in, leadership gets a recurring-revenue view per account. See [Recurring Service Contracts](/docs/whats-possible/service-dashboard-upgrades) for the upgrade.

These aren't dead weight — they're scaffolding. As each upgrade in the [What Else Is Possible](/docs/whats-possible) section lands, more of these zeroes turn into live numbers.

## A small sync gap to flag

**Staff Members** has only 2 records in the CRM, but the schema is full (Availability, Certifications, City, Region, Role, plus a Services Dashboard ID field that's the bridge key). The Service Dashboard already has the full staff roster — it just isn't mirrored yet. This is a one-day fix to populate, not a rebuild. Once it lights up, every event on the CRM Events tab will show the right tech assignments, and account managers can see which technicians work each venue without leaving the CRM.

## How the numbers grew

For comparison: at the start of April 2026 the CRM had **8,360 deals**. Today it has **9,994** — about **1,600 new deals** added in three weeks of normal sales operation. The CRM is keeping up with the actual rate of business.

## What this proves for the meeting

- **Real scale**: 20,090 people, 150,000 events, 28,000 time entries — this is not a sandbox.
- **Account-centric**: every record above hangs off a Company (or rolls up to one). One screen, one client.
- **Live and current**: the deal count grew by nearly 20% in three weeks.
- **Built for the future**: 14+ scaffolded objects are ready for the next features without schema work.
